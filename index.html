What is BMI?

Body Mass Index (BMI) is a number that compares your weight to your height. It is calculated by dividing your weight in kilograms by your height in metres squared (kg/m²). Doctors and public-health agencies use it as a quick screening tool because all it needs is a scale and a tape measure.

BMI categories

The standard adult categories are:

  • Below 18.5: Underweight
  • 18.5 to 24.9: Normal
  • 25 to 29.9: Overweight
  • 30 and above: Obese

Limits of BMI

BMI does not measure body fat directly. It cannot tell muscle from fat, and it ignores age, sex, and where fat is stored. A muscular athlete can land in the overweight range, while someone with little muscle can have a normal BMI and still carry excess body fat. Health guidelines for several Asian populations use lower cut-offs than the standard ones, because health risks can start rising at lower BMI values. For a fuller picture, pair BMI with a waist measurement or a body fat estimate.

What to do with your result

Treat your result as a starting point, not a diagnosis. If it falls outside the normal range, or you are worried about your weight, talk to a doctor or a registered dietitian. You can also estimate your daily energy needs with the BMR and calorie calculator or see a reference weight on the ideal weight calculator.

Frequently asked questions

How is BMI calculated?

Divide your weight in kilograms by your height in metres squared. For example, 70 kg at 1.75 m gives 70 ÷ 3.0625, which is about 22.9.

Is BMI accurate for athletes?

Not always. Muscle weighs more than fat, so very muscular people can have a high BMI without excess body fat. Measurements such as waist size or body fat percentage give better context.
