What is BMR?

Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR) is the number of calories your body burns at complete rest to keep you alive: breathing, circulation, cell repair, and temperature control. This calculator uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, which is widely used in nutrition practice:

  • Men: 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) − 5 × age + 5
  • Women: 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) − 5 × age − 161

From BMR to daily calories

Your BMR is multiplied by an activity factor to estimate the calories you need to maintain your weight:

  • Sedentary (little or no exercise): × 1.2
  • Light activity: × 1.375
  • Moderate activity: × 1.55
  • Active: × 1.725

Using the result

The calculator also shows an example deficit and surplus of 500 calories per day. As a rough guide, a 500-calorie daily gap corresponds to about 0.5 kg of weight change per week, though real results vary from person to person. Avoid very low calorie intakes unless a healthcare professional is supervising. To see how your weight relates to your height, try the BMI calculator.

Frequently asked questions

How accurate is a BMR calculator?

It gives an estimate based on averages. Your true needs can differ by 10% or more depending on muscle mass, genetics, and health, so use the number as a starting point and adjust based on how your body responds.

Which activity level should I choose?

If you are unsure, pick the lower option. People tend to overestimate how active they are, which leads to overestimating calorie needs.

Why does the calculator ask for gender?

The equation uses a different constant for men and women because average body composition differs between the two groups.
